## Getting Started on a Task[^topic]

You might find when working with a strange and unfamiliar system, taking that first step can be the hardest. Here are a few ways you can trick your brain into working on something when your brain *really* does not want to cooperate. 

### Pomodoro Timers[^source]

The Pomodoro Timer is a technique in which you set a alternating timers for 25, and then 5, minutes. For 25 minutes, you work on the task to the best of your ability. You write, or clean the room, or wash the dishes, or answer emails, whatever the case may be, for 25 minutes. Then, you take a 5 minute break, check on your progress, and evaluate what you may need to do next. Then the cycle repeats, 25 minutes working and 5 minutes of break, for as long as you want/need to work. By setting a timer, your brain can take comfort in the idea that the task *will* be over in a set amount of time. This can help what would otherwise be an overwhelming task feel more manageable, and shake off some of that paralysis.

A small modification that makes this technique even more effective, if applicable, is to join up with a small study group and work on your individual tasks in the vicinity of each other. While working, don't speak to each other to avoid distracting each other. Wear headphones if it helps you focus on your task instead of the others. During the 5 minute breaks, you can talk to each other and encourage each other on your progress.

### Music Challenge[^source]

Similar to the Pomodoro Timer, this method gives your brain a set amount of time to work on a task, which can help to ease yourself into working on the task. This technique revolves around putting on a song, playlist, or album, and rushing to get as much of the task done as you can before the music runs out. This method can feel more natural for people who are more musically inclined, but they can also be more distracting than silence if you are susceptable to that. It is highly recommended that, if you choose to try this, you choose music that is comforting, and that you know like the back of your hand, so that you can tune it into the background while you work on the task.

### Identify Roadblocks[^source]

This technique is best suited to times when you are in the mood to complete the task, you're ready to complete the task, and then as soon as you sit down to actually do it you freeze. Instead of sitting there paralyzed about the task, take some time to shift your focus from the task itself to what's *stopping* you from starting/working on the task. Write down those roadblocks, and then think about potential solutions. Take notes, and then the next time you sit down to work, you can reference those notes and stop yourself from spiraling.

### "I Will Refactor This Later"[^source2]

"Perfect is the enemy of Good", or so the saying goes. Often you can justify procrastinating on a task to yourself by telling yourself that you're "still planning" or "still finding the best way to approach it." This is the devil speaking. You can absolutely start working on the task before you find the perfect sequence of steps that will lead to the perfect end result. 
For this technique, identify the difference between "Must-haves" and "Nice-to-haves" for your end product. Whittle down the task into the base elements that make it up and focus on getting a core framework down. Even if that framework is something like an outline or storyboard, and nothing close to what you wanted to end up with, it will still help to have *something* to show for all the time you put in.
